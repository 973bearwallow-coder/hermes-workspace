# Amy + MoA Demo Package

## Goal
Create a short screen-share demo that shows:
- what Amy is,
- what the voice/assistant stack is doing under the hood,
- which models are being used,
- what bridge/routing layer ties it together,
- and why the design choices make sense.

This is meant to be **showable**, not just explainable.

---

## Core message
**Amy is the friendly front door.**
Behind her is a local/remote hybrid assistant stack that uses the right model for the right job, with voice and routing handled by local services we control.

The point is not “look, we have an app.”
The point is:
- we know what each layer does,
- we can explain why it exists,
- and we can change pieces without breaking the whole thing.

---

## Recommended format
A short demo package with three parts:
1. **Recorded walkthrough** — polished, repeatable, easy to replay.
2. **Live click-through** — one or two real actions to prove it works.
3. **Plain-English explanation** — why these model and bridge choices were made.

If time is short, prioritize the recorded walkthrough and save the live demo for one or two simple proof points.

---

## Audience assumptions
The audience is likely to care about one or more of these:
- “How does it actually work?”
- “What models are you using?”
- “Why did you build it this way?”
- “Can it be maintained?”
- “What makes it better than a generic chatbot?”

So the demo should emphasize **architecture, control, and judgment** rather than just UI polish.

---

## Recommended demo length
- **Short version:** 4–6 minutes
- **Normal version:** 7–10 minutes
- **If live questions start:** let the architecture slide become the anchor

---

## Demo outline

### 1) Opening hook — 20 to 30 seconds
Say something like:

> “This is Amy — the voice front door. What you’re seeing is not just a chatbot; it’s a stack that routes speech, memory, models, and assistants in a way that keeps the system useful and controllable.”

What to show:
- Amy interface or entry point
- a simple “hello” or “what can you do?” interaction

---

### 2) Show the voice layer — 45 to 60 seconds
Explain:
- Amy is the human-friendly voice layer.
- Voice input is captured locally.
- Speech is transcribed before it reaches the reasoning layer.
- Output speech is generated by the local voice stack.

Use this plain-English summary:

> “We don’t send everything through one opaque service. We split the job into speech capture, transcription, reasoning, and speech output. That gives us more control and better debugging.”

Possible talking points:
- local voice tools are faster to inspect
- the system is easier to debug when each layer is separate
- if one part fails, we know where to look

---

### 3) Show the model routing — 60 to 90 seconds
Explain that different tasks can use different models.

Suggested explanation:

> “We use a model-routing approach so we aren’t paying the same cost or latency for every task. Fast or routine tasks can use a cheaper/faster model, while higher-value reasoning can be escalated when it actually matters.”

What to mention:
- the MoA layer exists to pick the right model for the job
- we don’t want a single model pretending to be perfect at everything
- cheaper models are fine for many tasks
- the stronger models are reserved for harder or more important work

If you want to name the current stack, use this framing carefully:
- **Atlas brain / daily driver**: the current configured baseline
- **MoA routing**: the orchestrator that decides when to escalate
- **high-value reasoning**: reserved for difficult or important tasks

If you show exact model names, keep it simple and only mention the ones you are comfortable defending in the room.

---

### 4) Show the bridge / plumbing — 45 to 60 seconds
Explain the “bridge” as the glue that makes it usable.

Plain-English version:

> “The bridge is the part that connects the user-facing assistant to the actual services under the hood. It handles routing, hands off to the right model or voice service, and keeps the system organized instead of brittle.”

Good things to mention:
- why the bridge exists
- why we don’t want the UI and models tightly fused
- why local services are easier to fix than a giant monolith
- why a clear bridge helps reliability and debugging

---

### 5) Explain why we built it this way — 60 to 90 seconds
This is the credibility section.

Say something like:

> “The design is intentionally modular. The goal is not just to get answers — it’s to keep the system understandable, cheap enough to run, and easy to improve without rewriting everything.”

Key reasons:
- lower cost
- better control
- better privacy
- easier debugging
- easier to swap models later
- easier to keep the assistant honest about what it is actually doing

---

### 6) Finish with a real interaction — 30 to 60 seconds
Do one live action that proves the system is working.

Good choices:
- ask Amy a simple question
- ask for a quick summary of the architecture
- ask it to explain the voice path in one sentence
- ask MoA to route a routine question and then a harder one

Keep it short and visible.

---

## Shot list

### Shot 1 — Title / intro
- show Amy open
- quick intro line
- one normal question or greeting

### Shot 2 — Voice path
- show the mic / voice interface
- mention transcription
- mention local voice output

### Shot 3 — Model/routing explanation
- show the routing or configuration view
- explain that different tasks go to different models
- mention the cost/value rationale

### Shot 4 — Bridge explanation
- show the service/bridge or dashboard view
- explain why the bridge matters

### Shot 5 — Live proof
- ask a real question
- show the answer come back
- optionally compare routine vs harder routing

### Shot 6 — Close
- summary line
- why this matters
- end on the “we can control it” message

## Suggested talk track

### Short version
> “Amy is the front door. Underneath that, we have voice capture, transcription, model routing, and output generation split into separate parts. That’s deliberate: it makes the system easier to understand, cheaper to run, and easier to improve. The MoA layer helps decide whether a request should stay on a fast/cheap model or get escalated to a stronger one. The bridge is what keeps the whole thing connected without turning it into one giant fragile app.”

### Slightly more technical version
> “We separate the assistant into a voice layer, a reasoning layer, and a routing/bridge layer. Voicebox handles the local voice side, the model stack handles response generation, and MoA decides how to spend compute wisely. That gives us a system that’s modular instead of monolithic.”

### Friendly version for non-technical people
> “Think of Amy as the person at the front desk, not the whole building. The building behind her has different rooms for listening, thinking, deciding, and speaking. That’s what makes it useful instead of just flashy.”

---

## What to emphasize
- This is **engineered**, not improvised
- We know **what each piece does**
- We can explain **why each piece exists**
- We can **swap or improve parts** without starting over
- We prefer **judicious use of models**, not model-spam

---

## What to avoid
- don’t overlong technical deep dives unless asked
- don’t drown people in model names
- don’t imply every part is perfect
- don’t make it sound like magic
- don’t present the bridge as if it’s the product; it’s the plumbing

---

## If you want the demo to feel polished
Use this order:
1. human-facing intro
2. one live interaction
3. architecture explanation
4. routing / model choice explanation
5. one more live proof
6. close with why it matters

That order keeps people engaged and proves the system is real.
